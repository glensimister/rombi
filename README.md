# ROMBI - Rapid  Open Modular Building Initiative
      
<p>ROMBI consists of modular panels that can be made with practically no skills. There are some good modular framing systems out there, such as V-slot and T-slot, but they are typically meant for engineers. They are too heavy, too expensive, and relatively inaccessible to the average person. I came up with this system to make modular construction fast, affordable and accessible.</p>

## Potential Use Cases

ROMBI is intended as a flexible, modular construction system rather than a solution for one specific product. The following are examples of potential applications, ranging from simple enclosures to more ambitious projects.

This is an evolving list. Some ideas are relatively straightforward, while others would require additional components, testing, specialist knowledge or further development. The aim is to encourage experimentation, adaptation and contributions from the community.

| Potential Application | Description | Additional Considerations |
|---|---|---|
| **Office Partitions & Room Dividers** | Quickly create a space within a space. | - 
| **Meditation & Sensory Booths** | Personal spaces for meditation, relaxation and concentration. Could incorporate controlled lighting, speakers, music and interactive visuals. Could also provide a physical setting for experiences such as ZenBlocks, a relaxing concentration game. | Lighting, ventilation, acoustic treatment and integrated electronics. |
| **Hydrotherapy & Sensory Deprivation Enclosures** | Enclosures designed around baths or flotation tanks, potentially incorporating relaxing music, lighting and visual effects before and after sessions. | Specialist waterproofing, electrical safety, humidity control and hygiene requirements. |
| **Cinema, Gaming & VR Booths** | Compact personal entertainment spaces with tablet or monitor mounts, headphones or surround sound, lighting and optional projection or immersive visual effects. Could also accommodate shared viewing experiences. | Display positioning, acoustics, ventilation and equipment mounting. VR applications would need sufficient clearance and safe movement space. |
| **Children's Mini-Cinemas & Hideaways** | Small, elevated personal spaces for children to watch films, play games, read or relax. Could include blackout curtains, tablet holders, lighting and optional speakers. | Child safety, ventilation, stable construction and safe access. |
| **Personal Focus & Video-Call Booths** | Compact private spaces for homework, remote working, video calls and online meetings. Could provide a controlled background, lighting, tablet or monitor mounting and some acoustic treatment. | Privacy, ventilation, lighting and realistic expectations regarding sound isolation. |
| **Vocal, Voiceover & Podcast Booths** | Enclosures for recording vocals, voiceovers, podcasts and other audio. Interchangeable acoustic panels could help adapt the space to different recording requirements. | Acoustic treatment, ventilation and sound isolation. |
| **Photo & Content-Creation Booths** | Compact booths for photography, video recording, streaming and content creation. Could incorporate cameras, lighting, interchangeable backgrounds and equipment mounts. | Lighting control, camera positioning and background materials. |
| **Mobile DJ & Karaoke Booths** | Portable entertainment enclosures for events, parties and temporary installations. Could provide a dedicated space for DJs, karaoke equipment and lighting effects. | Transportability, stability, ventilation, electrical safety and weather protection for outdoor use. |
| **Pet Shelters & Animal Enclosures** | Modular shelters for cats and dogs, including outdoor cat houses and dog-waiting stations outside shops. Could also be adapted for rabbits, guinea pigs, snakes and other animals with suitable environmental requirements. | Animal welfare, ventilation, temperature control, cleaning, weatherproofing and species-specific requirements. |
| **Livestock & Veterinary Recovery Enclosures** | Specialised enclosures for animals recovering from illness or injury, or for newborn animals requiring a controlled environment. | Veterinary input, hygiene, temperature regulation, ventilation and animal welfare validation. |
| **Kiosks & Exhibition Booths** | Compact, configurable structures for museums, exhibitions, information points, demonstrations and interactive displays. | Accessibility, public safety, display mounting and ease of assembly. |
| **3D Printer & Equipment Enclosures** | Enclosures for 3D printers and other workshop equipment, providing protection, temperature management and potentially noise reduction. | Heat management, ventilation, fire safety and access for maintenance. |
| **Generator Enclosures** | Outdoor housings for generators and other machinery, providing weather protection and potentially reducing noise. | Exhaust ventilation, heat dissipation, fire safety and manufacturer-specified clearances. |
| **Server & Network Enclosures** | Modular cabinets or enclosures for servers, networking equipment and other electronics, potentially incorporating acoustic treatment. | Cooling, airflow, cable management, fire safety and equipment access. |
| **Food Fermentation & Proofing Chambers** | Insulated enclosures for maintaining controlled temperatures during bread proving, fermentation and other food-production processes. | Temperature regulation, humidity, food-safe materials and cleaning. |
| **Hydroponic & Growing Chambers** | Enclosures for indoor growing, hydroponics and other applications requiring a controlled environment. | Lighting, humidity, ventilation, water management and electrical safety. |
| **Battery Energy Storage Enclosures** | Housings for battery systems, including domestic solar energy storage, where environmental conditions need to be managed. | Fire safety, thermal management, ventilation and compliance with battery manufacturer's requirements. |
| **Emergency & Earthquake Shelters** | Personal protective enclosures intended to provide emergency shelter or protection during earthquakes and other disasters. | Requires specialist structural engineering, rigorous testing and independent safety validation before any protective claims can be made. |

These examples are not exhaustive. The modular nature of ROMBI means that components can potentially be adapted, combined or redesigned to suit applications that have not yet been explored.

**Community contributions and new ideas are welcome.**

### Holographic Video Conferencing Display

A modular desk-mounted telepresence display that creates the illusion of a remote participant appearing as a floating image, rather than on a conventional computer screen.

The concept uses two connected modular sections: one accommodates the user's head and webcam, while the other houses an optical display. A phone, tablet or small screen displays the remote participant against a black background. An angled transparent reflector, potentially using beam-splitting film, reflects the image towards the viewer, creating a Pepper's Ghost-style illusion.

Rather than constructing an expensive, fully enclosed booth, the initial design could be a compact desk add-on. An elevated ROMBI cube could sit partly on the desk and extend beyond its edge, positioning the optical display at a comfortable viewing height.

Potential features include:

- **Floating video image:** The remote participant appears to occupy a physical space rather than a conventional flat screen.
- **Modular optical components:** Interchangeable reflectors, beam-splitting materials and display devices allow experimentation with different optical arrangements.
- **Independent webcam:** A separate camera captures the local participant and connects to the video-call device.
- **Compact design:** A desk-mounted accessory avoids the cost and space requirements of a complete booth.
- **Adaptable applications:** Potential uses include business meetings, remote consultations, education, reception desks and personal communication.

**Additional considerations:** Achieving convincing image brightness, clarity, viewing angles and apparent eye contact will require experimentation. The optical display needs a dark background, and the video feed must be formatted appropriately for reflection. Both participants would ideally have compatible displays to create a two-way telepresence experience. The initial prototype should focus on proving the optical effect before developing the complete desk-mounted structure.
