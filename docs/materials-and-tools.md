## Equipment, Materials and Indicative Cost per L Panel

### Introduction

Modular construction systems can involve a higher initial material cost than conventional building methods because they require standardised components, specialised connectors and carefully designed interfaces. However, these upfront costs can potentially be offset by faster assembly, reduced labour, easier maintenance, and the ability to dismantle, reuse and reconfigure components.

As ROMBI develops, costs should decrease through design improvements, bulk purchasing, alternative suppliers and more efficient manufacturing methods. For further information, see **[Principles of Modular Design and How ROMBI Addresses Them](#principles-of-modular-design)**.

The following figures are **rough estimates based on current prototype materials and example retail prices**, not a fixed bill of materials or a final quotation. Actual costs will vary according to supplier, location, availability, quantity purchased, manufacturing method and future design revisions.

Readers are encouraged to source materials locally, manufacture components themselves where practical, or use third-party services. Optional equipment and accessories are listed separately and are excluded from the core estimate.

This subtotal includes the estimated cost of the specified quantities of priced components, including the PETG filament, but excludes the additional hardware whose quantities or costs have not been fully established. The actual amount spent purchasing materials may be higher because some components are sold only in packs. Conversely, bulk purchasing, alternative suppliers, discounted materials and in-house manufacturing may reduce the effective cost.

**This is an early-stage estimate for the current prototype, not a final production cost.** A more accurate figure will be published when the design, bill of materials and manufacturing process are sufficiently stable.
